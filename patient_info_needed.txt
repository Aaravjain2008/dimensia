1. Basic & diagnostic context

Age, sex
Type of dementia (if diagnosed) and current stage (early/middle/late)
Any doctor's notes/prescription details relevant to daily function
Known comorbidities (diabetes, hypertension, etc. — affects fatigue, meal timing, medication interplay)

2. Cognitive & functional baseline

Domain-by-domain strengths/weaknesses: memory, attention, recognition, language, problem-solving
MMSE/MoCA score or rough clinician assessment, if available
Level of independence: what they can do alone vs. what needs supervision

3. Sensory & physical constraints

Hearing status (needs voice loud/slow? hearing aid?)
Vision status (needs large text/high contrast?)
Mobility/motor limitations (can they hold a tablet, tap accurately, walk unassisted?)
Literacy level in their language

4. Daily rhythm

Wake time, sleep time, nap habits
Meal times
Medication schedule (times, names — just for reminder purposes, not clinical dosing decisions)
Best/worst times of day for engagement (many patients have "sundowning" — worse in late afternoon/evening)

5. Personal & cultural content (for reminiscence activities)

Names and photos of close family members
Familiar places (hometown, landmarks)
Favorite songs, festivals, foods
Preferred language/dialect

6. Behavioral & safety flags

Known triggers for agitation or confusion
History of wandering or getting lost
Emergency contacts
Any past safety incidents (falls, etc.)

7. Caregiver-side info

Who the primary caregiver is, and their availability (hours/day realistically available)
Caregiver's comfort with technology
Caregiver's current stress/burden level (even a rough self-rating)

8. Ongoing feedback (to be logged over time, not at intake)

Daily activity log: completed / skipped / enjoyed / agitated
Notes on what worked or didn't, day to day
